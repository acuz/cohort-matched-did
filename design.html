Simulation design

Population

Each replication draws a balanced panel of 1950 units observed over \(T = 12\) periods. Three cohorts are treated, first in periods 4, 7 and 10; a reservoir of never-treated units supplies the controls.

Group First treated Units \(X \sim\) \(\Pr(Z=1)\) \(\Pr(U=1)\)
Cohort 4 \(g = 4\) 150 \(N(0,\,1)\) 0.75 0.8
Cohort 7 \(g = 7\) 250 \(N(1.5,\,0.75^2)\) 0.50 0.5
Cohort 10 \(g = 10\) 350 \(N(3,\,0.5^2)\) 0.25 0.2
Never treated — 1200 mixture of the three cohort laws

The never-treated reservoir is built from three equal-sized blocks whose covariates follow the three cohort distributions, so that every cohort has suitable matches. Adjacent cohorts overlap in \(X\): the covariate is an informative but noisy predictor of a unit’s cohort, and the middle cohort overlaps with both neighbours. \(X\) and \(Z\) are observed; \(U\) is a latent characteristic that enters untreated outcomes in some designs but is never available to the estimators.

Outcomes

Untreated outcomes follow \[ Y_{it}(0) = \alpha_i + \lambda t + \mathrm{PT}_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}, \qquad Y_{it} = Y_{it}(0) + \mathbf{1}\{t \ge G_i\}\,\tau_{it}, \] with unit fixed effects \(\alpha_i\), a common trend \(\lambda t\), idiosyncratic noise \(\varepsilon_{it}\) and a design-specific trend component \(\mathrm{PT}_{it}\) that may depend on the unit’s characteristics.

Untreated trend designs

Design Trend term PT Description
No trend \(0\) Parallel trends hold unconditionally
Linear in X and Z \((0.2X+0.2Z)\,t\) Untreated trend linear in X and Z
Linear in X \(0.2\,X t\) Untreated trend linear in X only
Linear in Z \(0.2\,Z t\) Untreated trend linear in Z only
X × Z interaction \(0.2\,XZ\,t\) Untreated trend driven by the interaction X × Z
Quadratic in X \(0.1\,X^2 t\) Untreated trend quadratic in X
Latent U \(0.25\,U t\) Linear trend in a latent, cohort-correlated characteristic U
Step in X \(X\,\mathbf{1}(t\ge 8)\) Level shift from period 8 proportional to X
Step in latent U \(U\,\mathbf{1}(t\ge 8)\) Level shift from period 8 proportional to the latent U
Drifting X \(0.2\,X_{it}\,t\) Trend in X, with X drifting at cohort-specific rates after matching
Cohort-specific slopes \(\delta_g\, t\) Cohort-specific trend slopes unrelated to X

In the drifting X design, \(X_{it} = X_{i0} + \kappa_g (t-1)\) with cohort-specific drift rates, so that units matched on \(X\) at baseline diverge afterwards. In the cohort-specific slopes design each cohort, and the never-treated units drawn from its covariate law, has its own trend slope \(\delta_g\), unrelated to \(X\).

Treatment-effect patterns

Pattern Unit-level effect \(\tau_{it}\), \(e = t - g\) \(ATT(4,e)\) \(ATT(7,e)\) \(ATT(10,e)\)
Constant \(2\) \(2\) \(2\) \(2\)
Dynamic, cohort-heterogeneous \(a_g + d_g\, e\) \(1 + 0.5e\) \(1.5 + 0.75e\) \(2 + e\)
Covariate-dependent \(2 + b_g X_i\), \(b_g = 0.5, 0.75, 1\) \(2\) \(3.125\) \(5\)

The constant pattern isolates bias that comes from the comparison group. The dynamic pattern is the one reported in the paper’s Monte Carlo table. In the covariate-dependent pattern the effect varies with a matching covariate, so a cohort’s average effect depends on its covariate composition.

Matching

Treated units are matched one-to-one, without replacement, to never-treated units by nearest neighbour on the standardized covariates \((X, Z)\) measured at \(g-3\), before any treatment-related dynamics. Matching proceeds sequentially in order of treatment: cohort 4 first, then 7, then 10, each drawing from the controls left by earlier cohorts. Every matched control therefore carries the label of the cohort it was matched to; never-treated units that are not matched are dropped.

The simulation uses greedy nearest-neighbour matching within each cohort. The paper’s application chooses the within-cohort assignment to minimize the total covariate discrepancy; the two coincide whenever matches are not contested.

Estimators

Estimator Implementation Controls for cohort \(g\)
TWFE, matched sample Event-study regression with unit and period fixed effects on the matched sample Everyone else in the matched sample
CS, pooled matched sample did::att_gt, never-treated controls, no covariates, on the matched sample All matched controls, regardless of cohort
CM-CS (ours) att_gt_mc(match_cohort = TRUE) Only the controls matched to cohort \(g\)
CS, covariates did::att_gt on the full sample with linear covariates \(X\), \(Z\) (doubly robust) All never-treated units, reweighted by covariates

The last estimator does not use matching; it appears on the Matching vs. conditioning page. All CS-type estimators use a universal base period \(g-1\) and are aggregated to event time with cohort-share weights. Bias statistics average the event-study estimates over post-treatment periods \(e \ge 0\) across 100 replications.

Cuzzola & Tiedtke (2026), Not All Scars Are Equal: Job Loss Along the Wage Distribution, working paper.
